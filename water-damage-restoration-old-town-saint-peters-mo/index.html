Water Damage Restoration in Old Town, Saint Peters, MO

Old Town is the original center of St. Peters, set between Dardenne Creek and Spencer Creek north of Interstate 70. Here is how water damage is handled in an older district beside two creeks.

The Area

Where Old Town sits in St. Peters

Old Town St. Peters is the historic center of the city. The Missouri State Auditor describes the surrounding Old Town redevelopment area as bounded on the east by Spencer Creek, on the south by the Interstate 70 right-of-way, on the west by Dardenne Creek and on the north by the Corps of Engineers Old Town St. Peters Levee. That makes it a compact district defined by water on three sides and the highway on the fourth.

The district mixes older houses, small businesses and public parks. Old Town Park, the city's oldest park, and the Main Street corridor with its older buildings are the landmarks most residents use to describe the area. We do not claim an official boundary for the neighborhood beyond that redevelopment description.

Local Context

What the public records say about water here

The redevelopment plan for this area includes raising flood protection behind the Old Town levee from the 100-year level to the 500-year level. The U.S. Geological Survey published flood-inundation maps for Dardenne Creek in 2019 using the stream gauge at Old Town St. Peters, and the maps drew on documented high-water marks from the December 2015 flood.

The National Weather Service lists flood stage on Dardenne Creek at St. Peters as 18 feet. In a June 2025 flood statement it noted that at 19.2 feet, Lone Wolff Park begins flooding near Main Street and Highway C. The NOAA gauge also recorded a crest of 23.50 feet on July 26, 2022. These are records for the creek, not a list of damaged homes, and they do not say that any particular street flooded.

Water damage problems this area can see

Water damage in Old Town does not have to come from a creek. A supply line, a water heater, a clogged drain or a roof opening can soak a home or a shop in any district. First Steps

What to do if water is still spreading

STEP 1

Keep clear of the water

Creek water, sewer water and water near outlets can all be hazardous. Keep children and pets out, and never touch a breaker while standing in water.

STEP 2

Find the source

If a pipe or appliance is leaking, close its valve only when the floor around it is dry and the valve is easy to reach.

STEP 3

Raise valuables

Move papers, small furniture and keepsakes to higher shelves or a dry room, but leave heavy items for the crew.

STEP 4

Record what you see

Photograph rooms and damaged items from several angles and write down when the water appeared.

Contaminated water

Creek water and sewage can carry bacteria and debris. Do not wade through it, and do not rely on household fans or an ordinary vacuum. Call for professional help and keep children and pets away.

The Process

How extraction and drying work

The crew first finds where the water is and stops anything still feeding it. Standing water is pumped or extracted, then moisture meters and a thermal camera map the wet areas. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure while readings are logged each visit. Materials that cannot be dried are removed and listed. The industry reference for this work is the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, named here only as a yardstick for any plan you receive.

Older buildings add judgment calls. Plaster, old flooring and layered finishes dry at different speeds, so the first reading guides what stays and what goes. Ask for the scope in writing before work starts.

Claims

Insurance documentation

Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help describe the damage and the work. Coverage depends on the cause of the loss and your policy, so read the policy and contact your insurer. We do not decide coverage and cannot promise that a claim will be approved.

Common Questions

Old Town water damage questions

Is Old Town St. Peters in a flood zone?

Parts of the Dardenne Creek and Spencer Creek corridors carry FEMA base flood elevations for the City of St. Peters, but that does not place every street in a mapped zone. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center or ask the City of St. Peters.

Does flood insurance cover water that enters from the creek?

Water that rises from outside is usually treated differently from a burst pipe, and a standard homeowners policy often excludes it. Ask your agent which policies apply to your property before a loss happens.

What should I do first after a sewer backup?

Stay out of the water, keep children and pets away and call the line. Sewage is contaminated, and household fans or a standard vacuum are not suitable for it.

Can older buildings on Main Street be dried in place?

Often a structure can be dried in place when the water is caught early, but materials such as plaster, old flooring and insulation need to be judged one by one after a moisture reading.

Do you work on shops and offices as well as homes?

Yes. The same steps apply to a small business, and the commercial water damage page explains how downtime is handled.

First Steps

If Water Is Still Spreading

STEP 1

Stop the source

Close the supply valve to the appliance or fixture, or shut the main water valve if you cannot find the source.

STEP 2

Make the area safe

Keep out of standing water near outlets and the panel. Switch off power to wet rooms only from dry ground.

STEP 3

Protect what you can

Lift furniture, rugs and boxes off wet floors, and move valuables to a dry room.

STEP 4

Photograph everything

Take wide and close-up photos before cleanup. They support an insurance claim.

Local Facts

Cottleville facts that shape water damage work

Cottleville is one of the older towns in St. Charles County. The city history says the area was first settled by Captain Warren G. Cottle under a Spanish land grant in 1798, and the city was incorporated in 1853. The 2020 census counted 5,611 residents, so it is a small city beside much larger neighbors.

Highway N, the main corridor through town, follows the old Boone's Lick Road, and the city reports that road crossed Dardenne Creek near the original settlement. If you live near the creek, check the FEMA flood map and ask the city about floodplain rules. The city also completed a $1.2 million streetscape project in 2011 along Highway N in the Old Town area.

The city offices are at 5490 Fifth Street, and Old Town has its own historic district area and community improvement district on the city maps. Older buildings and newer subdivisions sit side by side here, so supply plumbing, roofs and foundations vary widely from one address to the next.

The Process

How the response works for Cottleville addresses

  1. You call the line and describe the source and location of the water.
  2. The dispatcher gives you shut-off and safety steps and sends a crew when one is available.
  3. The crew extracts standing water and maps moisture with meters and a thermal camera.
  4. Wet material that cannot dry is removed, and air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the rest to the S500 standard.
  5. You receive moisture logs and photos to support an insurance claim.

Scope and price are explained before work starts, and the dispatcher can talk through cost factors on the first call. Insurance often covers sudden losses, while slow leaks are treated differently, so ask your insurer what applies.

Common Questions

Cottleville water damage questions

Do you cover every ZIP code in Cottleville?

Yes. We serve addresses in 63338, 63304 and 63376. The 63338 ZIP is tied to Cottleville post office boxes, while street addresses in and around the city use 63304 and 63376, the latter shared with Saint Peters. Give the dispatcher your street address and it is confirmed on the call.

Is emergency water removal available in Cottleville?

Yes. The 24/7 line takes calls at any hour, and crews are sent as soon as one is available. The dispatcher walks you through shutting off water and protecting belongings while you wait.

Does Cottleville have a city building department I should ask about repairs?

Cottleville is its own city with a city hall on Fifth Street, so ask the City of Cottleville about permits for plumbing or structural repairs. Restoration work is separate from the permitting question.

Can you work in the Old Town historic area?

Yes. Water damage in an older building is handled with the same extraction and drying steps. If the building is in the Old Town historic district, ask the city before changing exterior features during repairs.

How do I find out if my Cottleville home is in a flood zone?

Check the FEMA flood map for your address or ask the City of Cottleville. We do not state flood zones for specific addresses.

Hardwood Floor Water Damage Repair in Saint Peters, MO

Wood absorbs water and changes shape. Caught early, boards can be dried with floor mats. Left too long, they cup, buckle and darken. Find out which situation you are in.

The Problem

What water does to hardwood

Wood takes on moisture and releases it slowly. When a floor gets wet from a leak, a dishwasher, a pet accident or a basement, the boards swell, the finish clouds and the planks may cup or crown. The question is how fast the water is removed and how long the boards stay wet.

Callers often describe it as buckled floors after a leak, boards lifting at the seams, a floor with white cloudy patches or dark stains that will not clean. Each describes a different stage.

Cupping

Edges rise higher than the center. Often reversible if dried properly.

Crowning

The center rises above the edges, usually after cupping and sanding of wet wood.

Buckling

Boards lift off the subfloor. This is severe and usually means replacement.

Dark staining

Black marks under the finish often come from long exposure and mold.

Decision

Can the floor be saved or does it have to be replaced

The factors are water category, time, species and installation. Clean water caught early can often be dried with specialty floor mats that pull moisture out of the boards. Water that sat for days, contaminated water and glued-down floors on a wet slab are harder.

The Work

How a hardwood floor is dried

The crew extracts surface water, then measures moisture in the boards and subfloor. Floor drying mats are placed over the boards to pull moisture with controlled airflow, with dehumidifiers running to take the vapor out of the room. Readings are taken daily, and the floor comes off equipment when the boards return to the expected level.

If part of the floor must be replaced, the repair matches species, width and finish where possible. Refinishing comes after drying. The structural drying page explains why readings come first, and water extraction covers the step before. For kitchens, see kitchen water damage.

Sources

Common ways hardwood gets wet

Dishwasher and refrigerator leaks, plumbing leaks in a ceiling or wall, a failed water heater, a basement flood that wicks upward, and a slab leak that comes through the subfloor are the usual sources. A slab-related cause is covered on the slab leak page.

Cost

Cost factors and insurance

Cost depends on the square footage, the species and finish, whether drying works or boards need replacing and how long drying takes. Scope and price are explained before work starts. Mitigation and repair are separate pieces in an insurance claim.

Variables

Species, finish and installation matter

Wide planks move more than narrow strips, solid wood reacts differently from engineered flooring and glued-down floors on a slab dry differently from nailed floors over joists. A floor with a thick finish also dries more slowly because moisture escapes mostly through the edges and the underside.

Tell the dispatcher what you have if you know it. The crew checks the installation method during the first visit and chooses mats, airflow and humidity control to match.

Hardwood floors in Saint Peters

Safety

What to do before help arrives

  • Stop the source and wipe up surface water quickly.
  • Do not use heat guns or space heaters on the floor.
  • Do not cover the floor with plastic or rubber mats.
  • Remove rugs and furniture from wet boards.
  • Do not sand or refinish until a moisture reading says the floor is dry.

Common Questions

Hardwood floor water damage questions from Saint Peters homeowners

Can water-damaged hardwood floors be saved?

Sometimes. If the water was clean and drying started within a day or two, floor mats and dehumidifiers can often bring boards back. If boards have buckled or the finish has turned black, replacement is more likely.

Why is my hardwood floor cupping after a leak?

Boards absorb moisture from below, the bottom swells faster than the top and the edges rise. Cupping can flatten as the floor dries, so wait for a moisture reading before sanding.

Should I sand and refinish right away?

No. Sanding wet or damp wood traps moisture and can cause the floor to cup again. Sand only after readings show the boards and subfloor are dry.

Does insurance cover hardwood floor replacement?

Sudden leaks that damage flooring are often covered, while slow leaks over time often are not. Photograph the floor and the source, keep failed parts and ask your insurer.

Wet Mattress or Sofa After a Leak? What to Do in Saint Peters, MO

Upholstered furniture holds water deep inside. Whether it can be saved depends on the water category, how long it was wet and what is inside.

HOMEOWNER GUIDESAINT PETERS, MO
By the Saint Peters Water Damage Pros team

The short answer

A mattress or sofa soaked with clean water that is removed quickly can sometimes be dried. Furniture soaked with contaminated water, or left wet for days, usually cannot be saved. Foam, batting and padding hold water deep inside, and surface drying does not reach it.

Because the answer depends on the water category and time, start with those two questions.

Step 1: What kind of water was it?

Water category and what to do with soft furniture
CategorySource examplesTypical outcome
Category 1 (clean)Supply line, rain through a roofPossibly saved if dried quickly.
Category 2 (some contamination)Washing machine or dishwasher overflowOften discarded, depends on item and time.
Category 3 (gross contamination)Sewage, flood waterDiscard soft furniture.

Categories come from ANSI/IICRC S500. Time can make a Category 1 loss worse, because water that sits for a day or two picks up contamination. See our sewage backup page and flood page for Category 3 work.

Step 2: How long was it wet?

The EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to avoid mold growth. A mattress that has been wet for longer than that, or smells musty, is unlikely to be worth saving. Foam and fabric also stain and hold odors.

If you want to try drying clean-water furniture

  1. Move the item out of the wet area to a dry, ventilated room.
  2. Blot and press out as much water as you can with towels. Do not rub.
  3. Use a wet vacuum on the surface and pull water from the fabric.
  4. Stand the item on its edge or prop it so air reaches all sides.
  5. Run fans and a dehumidifier in the room.
  6. Check for musty smells after a few days. If they remain, discard the item.

Sunlight and fresh air help, but they do not replace airflow and dehumidification. A mattress is thick, and the core can stay wet while the surface feels dry.

Items that can often be cleaned and items that cannot

Often cleaned

Hard furniture, wood frames and metal frames, after surface cleaning and drying.

Sometimes saved

Leather and upholstered pieces exposed to clean water for a short time.

Usually discarded

Mattresses and box springs after Category 2 or 3 water, and pillows or cushions.

Always discarded

Anything that touched sewage or flood water.

Document before you dispose

Take photos of the item and its label, and write down what it cost and when you bought it. Your insurer may ask. Do not throw anything away until you have photographed it. Keep a sample or a clear label photo if the adjuster asks.

Deciding whether to replace or restore

Weigh the cost of replacement against the odds that drying will work. A budget mattress soaked with anything but clean water for any length of time is rarely worth the effort. A high-end sofa with a solid frame may be worth a professional upholstery assessment. For anything touched by sewage or flood water, replacement is the safe choice.

Mattress covers and protectors help for small spills but do not stop a flood. If you store mattresses in a basement or garage, raise them off the floor and wrap them in plastic.

Disposing of ruined furniture

Check with the City of St. Peters or your trash hauler about bulky item pickup rules before you set furniture at the curb. Wrap or bag wet items to avoid spreading odor and contamination. Photograph them first, and keep any labels the insurer asks for.

Odor and stains after drying

Even when a piece dries, odor can remain. Foam and batting hold smells, and stains from the water can set. If a cushion smells musty after a few days of airflow, the moisture is still inside. At that point replacement is usually better than repeated attempts, since a damp core invites mold.

Do not use heavy fragrance sprays to cover it up. They hide the signal that tells you the item is still wet.

Furniture and floods in Saint Peters basements

Basements in the Saint Peters area often hold sofas, mattresses and stored furniture, which are the first items lost when water enters. Raising furniture off the floor on blocks and storing mattresses upstairs reduces losses.

Common Questions

Local questions from Saint Peters homeowners

Can a wet mattress be dried with a hair dryer?

It does not reach the core. A hair dryer dries the surface, and the inside stays wet. For clean-water spills, extraction, airflow and dehumidification work better, and many mattresses are replaced anyway.

Does insurance cover furniture damaged by water?

Contents coverage is separate from structure coverage. Check your policy for personal property, document the items and ask your agent.

What about a wet sofa with a wood frame?

The frame may be saved, while cushions and padding are replaced. A professional upholstery cleaner can advise on the fabric.

Can a professional dry a mattress?

Some companies offer extraction and drying for upholstery. For clean water and fast action, it may work, but many mattresses are replaced because the cost of repair rivals a new one.

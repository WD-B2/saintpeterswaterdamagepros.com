What We Do

Water damage restoration in Saint Peters, MO, from first call to final repair

Water damage restoration is the work of stopping the source, removing standing water, drying every wet material and then repairing what could not be saved. Done quickly, it keeps a wet room from turning into a rebuilt room. Done slowly, water wicks into drywall, subfloor and insulation, and mold gets a foothold.

Saint Peters Water Damage Pros runs a 24/7 phone line for homes and businesses in Saint Peters and the nearby towns of Cottleville, Earth City and Bridgeton. A dispatcher asks what the water is doing, tells you what to shut off, and sends a restoration crew as soon as one is available. Same-day response is the goal, and the dispatcher gives you a realistic window rather than a promise.

The industry standard for this work is ANSI/IICRC S500, which defines water categories, damage classes and drying goals. You can ask any company, including ours, whether its crews work to it. The same applies to S520 for mold. If you want the details on any one problem, start with the page for the source of your water, such as a burst supply line, a failed water heater or a sewage backup.

Emergency Steps

What to do in the first hour while you wait for the crew

Water damage is an emergency from the first minute because every hour adds more wet material. These steps are what a dispatcher would tell you on the phone. Do only the ones that are safe for you to reach.

  1. Stop the source if you can. For a supply line, turn off the main water valve, usually where the line enters the house near the water meter.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water. If water is near outlets, panels or appliances, stay out and call the utility or 911 first.
  3. Lift valuables, electronics, papers and rugs off wet floors. Pull cushions and boxes out of reach of the water.
  4. Keep people and pets out of any water that came from a drain, a toilet or outside. That water is contaminated until proven otherwise.
  5. Take photos and a short video of the damage before anything moves. Insurers ask for them.
  6. Call the 24/7 line at (636) 747-8964 and describe what you see.

Never wade into water that touches electricity

If water is covering outlets, a breaker panel or a running appliance, do not enter the room. Call the electric utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before you call us. Restoration can wait a few minutes. A shock cannot be undone.

First 72 Hours

What happens after you call, and why speed matters

The first three days decide how much of the structure can be saved. Wet drywall and carpet pad start to break down quickly, and the EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to head off mold growth. A typical job moves through six stages.

Step 01

The call and the safety check

The dispatcher asks where the water is coming from, how far it has spread and whether power or sewage is involved. You get shut-off and safety steps before anything else.

Step 02

Assessment and moisture mapping

The crew finds the wet areas with moisture meters and, where useful, a thermal imaging camera. Water is sorted into a category and the damage into a class under S500, which sets the drying plan.

Step 03

Extraction

Pumps, wet vacuums and extraction units pull out standing water and water held in carpet. Removing water as liquid is far faster than drying it as vapor.

Step 04

Removal of what cannot dry

Saturated pad, ruined insulation and drywall that has gone soft are cut out so the cavity can breathe. Contaminated materials always come out.

Step 05

Structural drying

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run for days while moisture readings are logged. The equipment stays until the readings reach the dry standard for that material. See structural drying and dehumidification for how this works.

Step 06

Repair

Once everything reads dry, drywall, paint, flooring and trim go back. Repair scope and price are explained before the work starts.

Repair

Water damage repair in Saint Peters after the drying is done

Drying and repair are separate jobs, and homeowners often want one team for both. Repair covers whatever the water changed: soft drywall, swollen baseboards, cupped hardwood, a stained ceiling or carpet that could not be saved. The wall and drywall page covers cut-outs and finish work, and the hardwood floor page explains when boards can be dried in place and when they cannot.

Ceilings deserve a mention of their own. A stain that keeps growing usually means water is still arriving from a roof, an upstairs bathroom or an attic duct. The ceiling water damage page covers that, including roof leaks, because the repair starts with finding where the water came from.

What drives the cost of a water damage job

Nobody can quote a fair number over the phone without knowing what the water touched. These are the factors that move the price. When you call, the dispatcher can talk through them, and scope and price are explained before work starts.

Insurance works the same way everywhere in general terms. Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, is often covered. Gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood damage from outside water is usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer, because only the policy decides.

Document everything for the claim

Photograph each wet room before cleanup, keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, save receipts for emergency costs and write down the time you noticed the water. Our crews log moisture readings daily, and that record helps support a claim. We never promise a claim will be approved.

Larger losses, where several rooms or floors are affected, benefit from an early written scope. Ask for it on the first call so you and your adjuster are looking at the same numbers.

Local Conditions

Creeks, storms and older roofs: what water does in Saint Peters

Saint Peters sits in St. Charles County close to Dardenne Creek, and that creek has a documented history.

The NOAA stream gauge at Dardenne Creek in Saint Peters lists flood stage at 18 feet and a record of crests that includes 23.50 feet on July 26, 2022, a major flood level. NOAA also logged crests of 21.42 feet in November 2024 and 21.43 feet in April 2025. If your home or business is near the creek, check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about flood coverage before the next storm.

The July 2022 event was extreme. The National Weather Service reported upwards of 11 inches of rain over roughly eight hours along an axis running from Hawk Point to Saint Peters, and NOAA recorded 12.34 inches at Saint Peters. For homeowners the lesson is practical. Heavy rain overwhelms sump pumps, floor drains and gutters at the same moment, so check that your pump has a battery backup and that downspouts discharge well away from the foundation.

Fall storms matter too. NWS St. Louis spotter and station reports on November 5, 2024 showed storm totals between about 4 and 6.7 inches around Saint Peters. Wet ground like that pushes water through basement walls and window wells even when no creek is involved. Our basement water damage page covers seepage and sump failure, and the storm water damage page covers wind-driven rain that gets in through the roof and siding.

Closer to the daily routine, plenty of water damage here starts indoors. Supply lines, water heaters and washer hoses fail in homes of every age from the Mexico Road corridor to the neighborhoods near City Centre Park and the St. Peters Rec-Plex. Rabbit Run Park, which sits on Dardenne Creek, is a reminder of how close ordinary neighborhoods are to the water.

Common Questions

Water damage questions from Saint Peters homeowners

How fast can someone get to my Saint Peters home?

Same-day response is the goal when crews are available, and the dispatcher gives you a realistic window on the call. We do not promise a fixed arrival time, because traffic on I-70 and the number of active jobs change it. While you wait, the dispatcher walks you through shutting off water and protecting belongings.

Do you handle water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Saint Peters?

Yes. The dispatch line is answered 24 hours a day, every day, including holidays. A burst pipe at 2 a.m. gets the same first steps as one at noon, and crews are sent as soon as one is available.

Will my homeowners insurance cover the damage?

It depends on the cause and your policy. Sudden and accidental damage, such as a burst pipe or a failed water heater, is often covered, and gradual leaks and flood water from outside often are not. Photograph everything, keep damaged items for the adjuster and ask your insurer what applies. We document moisture readings and scope, and we never promise approval.

How much does water damage restoration cost?

It depends on the water category, the area affected, the materials involved, how many days of drying are needed and how much has to be rebuilt. Scope and price are explained before work starts. Describe the situation on the phone and the dispatcher can tell you which factors apply.

Should I clean up the water myself before the crew arrives?

Stop the source, protect valuables and remove only clean surface water if it is safe. Do not run household fans over contaminated water, and do not pull up flooring or cut drywall before the damage is documented. Wet materials are heavy, and electrical hazards are easy to miss.

Is water damage restoration the same as basement waterproofing?

No. Restoration cleans up and dries what the water damaged. Waterproofing changes the foundation, drainage or grading so water stays out in the future. If your basement keeps taking on water, restoration handles the damage and a waterproofing or drainage specialist handles the cause.

How much notice do you need before someone can look at the damage?

Call the line and the dispatcher will tell you what is available today. For active leaks, same-day response when available is the goal. For older stains, a visit can usually be scheduled around your day, and the assessment explains what is wet and what is not.

How long does it take for mold to start growing after water damage?

Mold needs moisture, and the EPA advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to avoid growth. That is why the first two days matter so much. Our mold remediation page explains what follows when it has already started, including clearance testing.

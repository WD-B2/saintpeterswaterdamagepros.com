The Area

About Spencer Creek

Spencer Creek is an established residential subdivision area in St. Peters, recorded by the St. Charles County assessor under names such as Spencer Creek West Village. Residents describe it by its park, Spencer Creek Park, which sits in the middle of the subdivision, and by the creek trail that runs nearby.

Great Rivers Greenway and the City of St. Peters describe the Spencer Creek Trail as about 3.75 miles long, running from McClay Road to Mexico Road at the St. Peters City Centre. We use the park and trail as landmarks, not as an official neighborhood boundary.

Local Context

Housing and water in this area

Sales records for individual homes show houses built in the late 1970s and early 1980s, for example on Pilot Hill Drive and Spencer Trail. That is a sample of records, not a count of every home, and age alone does not tell you whether a house has had water damage.

FEMA lists base flood elevations for Spencer Creek and East Branch Spencer Creek within the City of St. Peters. The city also adopted a stormwater management policy in 2012 that refers to a city-wide Stormwater Master Plan completed in 2011, which studied flooding and stream stability across watersheds. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified.

Check your own address

A creek or a trail nearby does not mean a home is in a flood zone, and a home far from the creek can still have a leak. Look up your address on the FEMA map and ask your insurer what is covered.

Common ways water gets into a home

Most calls from a residential area like this start inside the house. A failed supply line, a washer hose, a dishwasher or a water heater can release water for hours before anyone notices. Heavy rain can also find a roof opening or a window. The pages below explain each type of response:

First Steps

What to do if water is still spreading

STEP 1

Make the room safe

Stay out of pooled water near cords, outlets and the panel, and keep pets away from the wet floor.

STEP 2

Close the valve

Turn off the supply to the leaking appliance or fixture, or the main valve if you cannot tell which one is failing.

STEP 3

Lift what you can

Pick up rugs, toys and boxes from wet flooring so they stop soaking up moisture.

STEP 4

Take photos

Capture the ceiling, walls and floor before anything is moved, and note the time you found the leak.

The Process

How a drying visit goes

A technician checks the affected rooms with a moisture meter, marks what is wet and explains the plan and the price before work starts. Standing water is extracted first. Air movers and dehumidifiers then run while the readings are logged each visit, and wet material that cannot be saved is removed and recorded.

Drying equipment runs for days, not hours. The goal is dry readings, not an elapsed time. The S500 standard is the industry reference for this work, and it is named here only so you can compare any plan against it.

Claims

Keeping records for your insurer

Take photos before moving anything, keep receipts for emergency steps and ask for the moisture readings and scope in writing. These records help describe the damage and the work. They do not guarantee payment, and coverage is decided by your insurer under your policy.

Common Questions

Spencer Creek water damage questions

Is my Spencer Creek home in a FEMA flood zone?

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. FEMA lists base flood elevations for Spencer Creek and East Branch Spencer Creek in the City of St. Peters, so look up your exact address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

Which school district serves Spencer Creek homes?

County assessor records for addresses on Spencer Valley Drive list the Fort Zumwalt School District, but boundaries vary by street, so confirm with the district for your address.

Do homes here have basements?

We do not state that. Basements and crawl spaces differ from house to house, so describe your home to the dispatcher and the right drying plan is chosen on site.

How is a slow leak behind a wall found?

A moisture meter and a thermal camera show wet material behind finished surfaces without opening everything. The crew then removes only what has to come out.

Will you tell me what my insurer will pay?

No. Coverage depends on your policy and the cause of damage, and your insurer decides. We can provide photos, readings and a written scope to help describe the work.

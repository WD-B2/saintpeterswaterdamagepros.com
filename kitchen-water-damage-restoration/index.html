The Problem

Kitchen water damage starts at the cabinet base

The kitchen is where water and wood meet most often. A leaking supply line, a failed drain or a loose dishwasher connection sends water into the cabinet base, along the toe-kick and under the floor. Kitchen water damage restoration in Saint Peters starts with finding out how far that water has traveled, because the visible puddle is rarely the whole story.

Callers describe it in plain terms: water under the sink, swollen cabinet bottoms, a floor that feels soft near the dishwasher, a musty smell in the cabinet. Each of those points to material that has been wet for a while.

Swollen cabinet base

The bottom panel or toe-kick bulges and the laminate peels at the edge.

Soft or discolored flooring

Vinyl lifts, laminate cups and tile grout darkens near the leak.

A musty cabinet smell

A smell that persists after drying usually means material is still wet.

Dripping or stained ceiling below

In a two-story home, water can travel through the floor to the ceiling below.

Options

Saving cabinets versus replacing them

Solid wood and plywood boxes tolerate moisture better than particleboard, which swells when soaked and rarely returns to shape. Countertops and doors are usually not the problem, since water attacks the bottoms of the boxes first. The decision depends on moisture readings, the cabinet material and how long the water stayed.

Drying behind and under cabinets takes specialized setups. A toe-kick can be removed or drilled so air movers push warm air into the cavity, and a floor-drying mat can dry flooring from the side. If the leak came from the dishwasher or refrigerator, appliance leak water damage covers that source.

The Work

How the kitchen is dried

The crew extracts standing water, checks the cabinet bases and subfloor with moisture meters and uses a thermal imaging camera to find hidden wet zones. Base trim and toe-kicks come off so air can reach the cavity. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run for several days while the readings are logged.

If the floor under the cabinets is saturated, a section may have to be opened. Hardwood or laminate in the kitchen follows the process on the hardwood floor page. The structural drying page explains why readings, not time, decide when equipment leaves.

Cost

Cost factors and insurance

The price follows the area affected, the cabinet material, whether the floor was wet and how many days of drying are needed. Scope and price are explained before work starts. Cabinet replacement is a repair cost rather than a mitigation one, and insurers treat the two differently.

Document the leak with photos, keep any failed parts and write down when you noticed it. A leak that was sudden and accidental is handled differently from one that ran slowly for months, so ask your insurer what applies.

Check them for drips twice a year, replace old compression lines and keep the cabinet under the sink clear enough to spot a leak early.

Safety

What to do before help arrives

  • Shut off the water supply under the sink or at the main.
  • Unplug appliances only if the outlet is dry and you are standing on dry ground.
  • Do not open a wet dishwasher or refrigerator panel.
  • Empty lower cabinets so the crew can inspect behind them.
  • Do not eat food that sat in contaminated water.

Common Questions

Kitchen water damage questions from Saint Peters homeowners

Can wet kitchen cabinets be saved?

Solid wood boxes dry better than particleboard, and early action matters. Particleboard and MDF swell and lose strength when soaked, so bases that stayed wet for more than a day or two are often replaced. A moisture reading tells you whether drying can work.

What should I do if I find water under the kitchen sink?

Shut off the supply valves under the sink, empty the cabinet and photograph the leak. Dry the area with towels, and call a plumber for the fitting and the restoration line if the cabinet base or floor is wet.

Will insurance cover cabinet and countertop damage from a leak?

Sudden leaks that damage cabinets are often covered, while slow leaks often are not. Photograph the damage, keep failed parts and ask your insurer what your policy covers.

Yes. The line is answered 24 hours a day. A burst line under the sink or a dishwasher leak that is still running is treated as urgent, and a crew is sent as soon as one is available.

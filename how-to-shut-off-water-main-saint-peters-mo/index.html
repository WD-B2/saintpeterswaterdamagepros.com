How to Shut Off the Water Main in Your Saint Peters, MO Home

When a pipe bursts, the fastest way to limit damage is to close the main valve. Find it now, before you need it.

HOMEOWNER GUIDESAINT PETERS, MO
By the Saint Peters Water Damage Pros team

Why this is the first thing to know

Water under supply pressure keeps flowing until you close a valve. Every minute of running water soaks more drywall and subfloor. Knowing where your main shutoff is and how to turn it can save thousands of dollars of damage. Most people look for it for the first time while water is on the floor, which is the worst moment.

Spend ten minutes this week finding it, testing it and labeling it. The rest of this guide shows you how.

Where to look

  • In the basement or crawl space, on the wall facing the street, where the water line enters the house.
  • In a utility room or garage, near the water heater.
  • In a closet or mechanical room on the first floor of homes without basements.
  • Outside, in a meter box near the curb, where the utility has a shutoff.

The main valve is usually near where the line comes through the foundation, often close to the water meter. If you cannot find it, check your home inspection report, ask the previous owner or ask a plumber to show you.

Two kinds of valve

Valve types and how to close them
ValveHow it worksTo close
Gate valveRound wheel handle, many turnsTurn clockwise until it stops. Older ones can stick or break.
Ball valveLever handle, quarter turnTurn the lever so it is perpendicular to the pipe.

Turn gate valves gently. Old ones can seize or leak at the stem. If a gate valve will not close, call a plumber and consider replacing it with a ball valve.

Steps after the water is off

  1. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the pipes and release pressure.
  2. Switch off the water heater if the broken line is on the hot side or if the tank has drained.
  3. Move belongings away from the wet area and photograph the damage.
  4. Call a plumber for the repair.
  5. Call the 24/7 restoration line to extract water and start drying.

If water is already on the floor, call (636) 747-8964 while you shut off the valve. The dispatcher can talk you through it. The burst pipe page explains what the crew does next.

Fixture shutoffs

Most sinks, toilets and appliances have their own small valves. Closing those stops a single leak without turning off the whole house. Turn them once or twice a year so they do not seize. If a valve is crusty or leaks when you turn it, replace it.

Before you travel

If you leave for more than a few days, especially in winter, consider closing the main valve. Some insurance policies have conditions about vacant homes and heat. Ask your agent. Leave the heat on, and open cabinet doors on exterior walls during a hard freeze.

Test and label your valves this week

Close the main valve fully, open a faucet to confirm the water stops, then reopen it slowly. If the valve is stiff, leaks at the stem or will not close all the way, call a plumber to replace it before you need it in an emergency. Tag the valve with a bright label and tell everyone in the house where it is.

Do the same for the water heater supply valve and for the shutoffs under sinks and behind toilets and appliances. A five-minute test can save a night of panic.

When the main will not close

If the valve is seized or leaking, do not force it with a wrench. Try the shutoff at the meter or curb if you have the key, and call the utility or a plumber right away. Keep calling the restoration line too, because extraction can start while the plumber is on the way.

Knowing your options ahead of time matters. A homeowner who has already found the curb stop and has the key can close it in a minute. One who has never looked loses precious time.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and S520 covers mold remediation. This page names them as references only, so you can compare any plan you receive against the same yardstick.

Cold snaps and the main valve

The National Weather Service St. Louis reported minus 6 degrees Fahrenheit at Lambert Airport on December 22 and 23, 2022, and minus 4 on February 16, 2021. Nights like that crack lines in unheated spaces, and a known main shutoff turns a flood into a repair. NWS climate data shows temperatures of 32 degrees or lower on fewer than 25 days in most years, so hard freezes are not annual, but they do come.

Common Questions

Local questions from Saint Peters homeowners

What if I cannot find the main valve?

Look for the meter, since the valve is often near it. If you still cannot find it, the utility can shut off the water at the curb. Call and explain it is an emergency.

Will shutting off the water damage anything?

No. Turn off the water heater if it will be drained, and open a faucet to relieve pressure. Open the main slowly when you turn it back on.

Who maintains the line from the street?

It depends on where the problem is. Ask the City of St. Peters or your water provider who is responsible for the line at your address.

Should I close the main when I go on vacation?

Many people do in winter or for long trips. Check your policy for vacancy conditions, and keep the heat on.

Water Damage Categories 1, 2 and 3 Explained for Saint Peters, MO

The category of water sets what can be saved and how the cleanup is done. Here is what each category means in plain terms.

HOMEOWNER GUIDESAINT PETERS, MO
By the Saint Peters Water Damage Pros team

Why categories exist

Not all water is equal. Water from a supply line is very different from water that came up through a sewer drain. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard sorts water into three categories based on the source and how contaminated it is. The category decides what can be dried and what must be removed.

A crew determines the category during assessment. You can also use the information below to understand what they tell you.

The three categories

Water categories under S500
CategoryDescriptionExample sourcesTypical handling
Category 1Clean water from a sanitary sourceSupply line, rain through a roof, sink overflow with clean waterExtract and dry. Many materials saved.
Category 2Significant contaminationDishwasher or washing machine discharge, toilet overflow with urine onlyExtract, remove porous materials, clean and dry.
Category 3Grossly contaminatedSewage, flood water, rising water from outsideRemove porous materials, disinfect, protective equipment.

Categories can get worse

Clean water becomes worse the longer it sits. After a day or two, Category 1 water can pick up contamination from the materials it touches and be treated as Category 2 or 3. That is another reason to act quickly. Temperature, time and contact with dirt all matter.

What changes at each level

Category 1

Carpet, pad and drywall can often be dried in place when caught early.

Category 2

Pad and carpet are often removed. Hard surfaces are cleaned and dried.

Category 3

Porous materials come out. Framing is cleaned and disinfected. Soft furniture is discarded.

Contents

Hard goods can be cleaned. Soft goods follow the same logic as building materials.

The classes of water damage, based on how much material is wet, are a separate scale. They guide the amount of drying equipment. Both the category and the class go into the scope of work.

What you can do safely

  • For Category 1, stop the source, extract water and call for drying.
  • For Category 2, avoid contact and call. Wear gloves and boots if you must enter.
  • For Category 3, stay out, keep children and pets away and call.
  • Photograph the water and the source before cleanup.

For Category 3 events, see sewage backup cleanup and flood damage. For clean-water events, see burst pipes and water heater leaks. To start, call (636) 747-8964.

How categories affect insurance

Category 3 events cost more because of removal and disinfection, and insurers may treat sewer backup and flood differently from clean-water losses. Ask your agent which categories your policy covers.

Classes of water damage, briefly

Alongside categories, the standard uses classes to describe how much moisture a loss involves. Class 1 is the least, with a small area and low-absorbing materials. Class 2 covers a larger area with wet carpet and cushion. Class 3 involves water from above that has soaked ceilings, walls and insulation. Class 4 covers deeply held moisture in dense materials such as hardwood and concrete.

The class guides how much equipment is used and how long drying takes. The category guides what can be saved. Together, they shape the scope.

Why the same job can change category

A clean supply line leak into a clean room is Category 1. If the water runs through a dirty crawl space or sits in a wet carpet for days, it can be reclassified. Crews reassess as they open walls and floors. The category written on the first day is a starting point, not a permanent label.

Ask your crew how they decided the category and what they will do if it changes. A clear answer is a good sign.

Examples from real household situations

ScenarioLikely category

Classification is a judgment, and an experienced crew explains theirs. When in doubt, the safer category wins, since the cost of being wrong is higher in the other direction.

Safety

What to do before help arrives

  • Treat water from sewage or outdoor flooding as contaminated.
  • Do not touch or wade through it without gloves and boots.
  • Keep children and pets out of the area.
  • Do not run household fans through contaminated areas.
  • Throw out food and porous items that touched it.

Categories in Saint Peters losses

Most losses inside Saint Peters homes start as Category 1, from supply lines, heaters and appliances. Storms and sewer events raise the category quickly. The rule is simple: treat anything that comes in from outside or up through a drain as contaminated.

Common Questions

Local questions from Saint Peters homeowners

Is rain water Category 1?

Rain falling through a roof is usually clean at first, but it often picks up dirt and insulation fibers. A crew decides based on what it touched.

Can I clean Category 2 or 3 water myself?

We do not recommend it. These categories involve contamination and need protective equipment and disinfection.

Who decides the category?

The restoration crew decides during assessment using the standard and the evidence, and your insurer may ask to see the reasoning.

Can I see the category in my paperwork?

Yes. Ask for it in the scope or the drying log. It is also useful to your insurer.

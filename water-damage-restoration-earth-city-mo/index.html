Overview

Earth City is a business district, so we answer it that way

Earth City is not a residential town. It is an unincorporated commercial area in St. Louis County along Interstate 70 near the Missouri River, built around a master-planned business park. A water loss here usually means a warehouse floor, an office suite, a loading dock or a mechanical room, not a basement.

If water is spreading through an Earth City building, call the 24/7 line at (636) 747-8964. The dispatcher will ask about the square footage, the source and what is stored on the floor, because inventory protection shapes the first hour.

Local Facts

Facts that matter for Earth City properties

  • Earth City is described as an unincorporated commercial area in St. Louis County, so there is no city hall to ask about building permits.
  • Industry listings describe the business park as fully developed, with roughly five million square feet of building space.
  • The Earth City Levee District was created by a St. Louis County Circuit Court decree in January 1994 and protects 1,891 acres.
  • Before the district existed, the Earth City Board of Trustees ran the flood control system under the business park indentures until February 1995.
  • The district states that four major floods since 1972, including the record 1993 flood, tested its system with minimal damage.
  • The district describes its levee as a 500-year levee managed around the clock by professional floodplain management firms.

None of that changes what a burst sprinkler line or a roof drain does inside a building. Flood protection stops river water, and the losses our line handles come from plumbing, roofs and equipment.

First Steps

If Water Is Still Spreading

STEP 1

Isolate the source

Close the zone valve, the sprinkler riser or the building main. Know where they are before you need them.

STEP 2

Protect stock and files

Move inventory onto pallets, lift files and electronics, and cover pallets that cannot move.

STEP 3

Control power

Shut off power to wet areas only from a dry panel. Call the utility or 911 if water reaches electrical equipment.

STEP 4

Document and notify

Photograph the loss, list affected stock and notify your landlord and insurer.

The Work

What commercial work looks like

Warehouses and offices differ from houses in the materials involved. Concrete slabs, metal studs, suspended ceilings and carpet tile all dry differently. The crew uses larger pumps and extractors, then moisture mapping, then air movers and LGR dehumidifiers sized to the volume of the space. Readings are logged each visit for the carrier.

Read the commercial water damage page for the full sequence, water extraction for the first step and structural drying for the equipment. Lease and landlord questions are covered on the commercial page.

Loss Types

Common Earth City loss types

Sprinkler or fire line failures

A broken head or a frozen line can release large volumes quickly.

Roof drain and flat roof leaks

Large roofs put water through ceilings onto stock and equipment.

Restroom and break room plumbing

Overflows and supply failures wet office suites.

HVAC condensate

Rooftop units and air handlers drip into ceilings. See AC leak damage.

Common Questions

Earth City water damage questions

Do you cover every ZIP code in Earth City?

Earth City is a single-ZIP area served by 63045. The nearby city of Bridgeton also uses 63045 for part of its addresses, so give the dispatcher your street address and it is confirmed on the call.

Is emergency water removal available in Earth City?

Yes. The 24/7 line takes calls at any hour, which matters for sprinkler failures and pipe breaks in buildings nobody is in overnight. Crews are sent as soon as one is available.

Is Earth City a city I can call for permits?

No. Earth City is an unincorporated commercial area in St. Louis County, so ask St. Louis County or your property manager about permits and building rules.

Does the levee protect Earth City from all flooding?

The Earth City Levee District describes a 500-year levee and flood control system. A levee does not stop plumbing failures, roof leaks or sprinkler discharges, which are the usual causes of loss inside a building.

Who do I notify first after a loss in a leased warehouse?

Notify your landlord or property manager and your insurer promptly, and read your lease for who handles the structure and who handles contents.

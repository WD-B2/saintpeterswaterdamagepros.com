When a sewage backup is an emergency

A sewage backup is an emergency because the water is grossly contaminated and spreads to anything it touches. If you see sewage in a drain, a toilet or a basement in Saint Peters, stop using water, keep people and pets out of the room and call the 24/7 line at (636) 747-8964.

  1. Stop using sinks, tubs, toilets, the washer and the dishwasher, since they all feed the same line.
  2. Keep children, pets and anyone not helping out of the affected area.
  3. Do not enter standing sewage if power is on or outlets are wet.
  4. Photograph the backup and the damaged belongings before cleanup.
  5. Call a plumber for the blockage and the restoration line for cleanup.
Symptoms

Signs of a sewer backup before it floods

Callers often describe the problem without the term. They say the toilet is gurgling, water is coming up in the basement floor drain, several drains are slow at once or there is a sewage smell in the lowest room.

Gurgling toilets and drains

Air pushed through traps by a blocked main line makes noise before water appears.

Water in a floor drain

Water rising in the lowest drain in the house means the line is backing up.

Several drains slow at once

One slow sink is local, but a tub, a toilet and a washer together point to the main.

Sewage odor in a lower room

A smell with no visible water can mean a trap is dry or the line has a leak.

The Work

How sewage cleanup works

Sewage is Category 3 water under ANSI/IICRC S500, so the crew works with protective equipment and containment. Liquid is extracted, and porous materials it touched, such as carpet, pad, drywall and insulation, are removed and bagged. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and treated with disinfectant.

The area is then dried with air movers and dehumidifiers, with readings logged until the structure is dry. See water extraction and structural drying for the equipment. If the water also came from outside, read about flood damage.

Local Note

Sewer backups in Saint Peters

Heavy rain can overload sewer lines and send water into the lowest drains. Basements and lower levels are the most exposed. Ask the City of St. Peters public works department who maintains the line at your address, and ask your insurer about a backup endorsement before you need one.

In nearby areas such as Bridgeton, the city manages its own sewer programs, so check with your city. Commercial properties should read the commercial water damage page.

Common Questions

Sewage backup questions from Saint Peters homeowners

Do you handle sewage backups at night or on weekends in Saint Peters?

Yes. The line is answered 24 hours a day. Backups often happen during heavy rain or at night, and a crew is sent as soon as one is available.

Why is water coming up through my basement floor drain?

A floor drain connects to the same line as other drains. Water rising there usually means the line is blocked or the main sewer is overloaded. A plumber clears the blockage and a restoration crew cleans up what came out.

Is a sewage backup covered by homeowners insurance?

Many policies exclude sewer backup unless a water backup endorsement was added. Check your policy and call your agent, and photograph everything before cleanup.

Can I clean up a small sewage backup myself?

Sewage is Category 3 under ANSI/IICRC S500 and requires protective equipment and disinfection. We do not recommend handling it yourself. Keep children and pets away and call.

Who is responsible for the sewer line, the city or me?

It depends on where the problem is. Ask the City of St. Peters public works department, since responsibility for lines on private property and in the street differs.

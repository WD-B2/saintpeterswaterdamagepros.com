When a commercial water loss is an emergency

A commercial water loss is an emergency from the first hour. Water ruins inventory, drops ceilings onto equipment and keeps staff from working. If your Saint Peters office, shop or warehouse is wet, call the 24/7 line at (636) 747-8964 and stop the source if you can do so safely.

  1. Shut off the water supply to the affected area or the main valve.
  2. Cut power to wet areas only from a dry panel, or call the utility.
  3. Move inventory, files and electronics out of the wet zone or onto pallets.
  4. Photograph the damage and make a list of affected stock.
  5. Notify your landlord, property manager and insurer.
Sources

What commercial losses have in common

Sprinkler and fire line failures

A broken head or a frozen line releases large volumes in a short time.

Supply and drain failures

Pipe breaks, restroom overflows and water heaters hit offices and shops.

Roof and storm leaks

Flat roofs and drains over warehouses leak into ceilings and stock.

HVAC condensate

Rooftop units and air handlers drip into ceilings and onto equipment.

The Work

How a commercial job runs

The crew starts with safety and extraction, using larger pumps and extractors than a house needs. Moisture mapping covers floors, walls, ceilings and any adjoining tenant spaces. Wet material is removed, and air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure to the S500 standard, with readings recorded by date.

Commercial buildings often have concrete slabs, metal studs and suspended ceilings, which dry differently from a house. See water extraction and structural drying. If the water came from outside, read about flood damage.

Cost

Cost factors, leases and insurance

Cost depends on the square footage, the water category, the building materials and the drying time. Scope and price are explained before work starts. Commercial claims bring in leases, landlords and business interruption terms, so notify all parties early.

Keep drying logs, photos, invoices and inventory lists in one place. We never promise claim outcomes.

Ask the dispatcher about staging. Many commercial jobs can be split into zones so part of the building keeps operating while another part is dried, and the plan should say which zone is which and when each reopens.

Continuity

Staying open while the structure dries

Business interruption is often the largest cost of a water loss. Ask for a staged plan that dries one zone at a time, protects the areas still in use and moves noisy work to quiet hours where possible. Tell the dispatcher which rooms matter most, such as a server closet, a cash office or a loading dock.

Carriers often ask for dates and readings to support a business interruption claim. The drying log shows when each zone was wet and when it reached the standard, which helps tie the loss to a timeline.

Safety

What to do before help arrives

  • Make sure everyone is out of any area with sagging ceiling tiles or standing water.
  • Shut off the water supply to the affected zone.
  • Cut power to wet circuits only if a qualified person can do it safely.
  • Do not move electronics or records through wet areas.
  • Document the loss with photos and a written timeline.

Common Questions

Commercial water damage questions from Saint Peters businesses

Do you handle commercial water emergencies at night or on weekends in Saint Peters?

Yes. The line is answered 24 hours a day, which matters for sprinkler failures and pipe breaks in unoccupied buildings. Crews are sent as soon as one is available.

Can you work around business hours?

Often yes. Scope and scheduling are discussed upfront so work can be staged to keep part of the building running where it is safe.

Who should I notify first, my insurer or my landlord?

Both, and promptly. Lease terms decide who handles the structure and who handles contents, so read the lease and call your carrier and landlord. Document everything.

What records do I need for a business claim?

Photos, moisture logs, inventory lists, invoices and a timeline. The crew provides drying logs, and you gather the business-side records.

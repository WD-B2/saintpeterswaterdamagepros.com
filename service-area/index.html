Property Types

Residential and commercial

Homes make up most of the calls in Saint Peters and Cottleville. Earth City is primarily commercial and industrial, and Bridgeton has both. The commercial water damage page explains how business losses are handled, including landlord and carrier notification.

Local Pages

What each community page covers

The Cottleville page covers Old Town, the ZIP codes in use and how permits and flood questions are handled by the city. The Earth City page covers the commercial district, its levee district and the kinds of losses that hit warehouses and offices.

The Bridgeton page covers the city's housing and business mix, its sewer lateral program and what to do when a drain backs up. Each page lists its own ZIP codes and answers questions specific to that community.

Edge of the Area

Calling from outside the listed towns

If you are in a neighboring town, say so on the call and give the street address. The dispatcher checks which crews are free and how soon one can reach you. Distance affects how quickly a crew can arrive, so a nearby address is usually reached sooner than one at the edge of the area.

In a widespread storm, every crew is busy and calls are worked in the order they arrive. The dispatcher still gives you shut-off and protection steps so you can limit the damage while you wait. Photograph everything and keep receipts for emergency steps.

Property Mix

Residential neighborhoods and business parks alike

Saint Peters and Cottleville are mostly residential, with subdivisions, older streets and retail corridors. Earth City is built around a business park, and Bridgeton mixes homes with distribution and office space. The first questions on a call change with the building, so tell the dispatcher what you have.

A house with a basement needs different equipment from a slab warehouse, and a finished office suite needs different protection from a loading dock. Describing the space helps the dispatcher send the right crew and tools the first time.

Prep

What to have ready when you call

Give the dispatcher your street address and nearest cross street, the source of the water if you know it, and how long it has been running. Mention whether the power is on in the wet area and whether anyone in the home has trouble moving around.

Photos from your phone help the crew plan equipment before arrival. If the water came from outside, note whether it is still rising.
